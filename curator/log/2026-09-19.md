## Learnings
- **2026-09-19 — The moon-date kill is the lunar analogue of the day-of-week kill, and it closes watches for free.** Any event whose name ties it to a moon phase must fall on the actual moon date (2026-07-19). 2026 full moons: ~Sep 26, ~Oct 26, ~Nov 24 (from the confirmed Aug 28 + 29.53d synodic month). A "FULL MOON" slug dated otherwise is a prior-year edition. This closed the Nov 19 watch and killed the new Sep 8 slug at zero search cost — the same pattern as the Sep 18 run, which closed three watches by weekday arithmetic. **Compute the moon/weekday check at the moment a slug is recorded, never at the watch window.** Two of the last two runs' best results came from arithmetic, not searching.
- **2026-09-19 — A live slug pattern with no current edition is evidence, not a null result.** The DTT and Pyramids of Chi per-edition patterns are both demonstrably indexed (four DTT editions, three PoC editions) yet neither has a Sep 26 2026 listing at D-7. That is much stronger evidence of a format shift to walk-in than a plain empty search would be, because it rules out "the pattern was never indexable". Distinguish "pattern absent" from "pattern present, edition absent" in every lunar sweep — the second one is a finding worth acting on.
- **2026-09-19 — Discovering a first-party URL is progress even when the URL is unreadable.** Two first-party pages were found today and both are egress-blocked. They still have value: they are legitimate `source_url`s for the day an event is confirmed, and they retire slug-guessing, which is where recent budget went. Log the first-party URL, then stop searching for that event's slug.
